How I think about risk, what I buy and why, and what I learn when I get it wrong.

BSc Economics & Finance student, University of Surrey. Updated September 2026.

Philosophy

Time horizon
Since starting my investing journey in 2024, my goal was to invest in long-duration structural trends, allocating capital towards innovation and the critical bottlenecks that define where the world is heading. Therefore, the ideal hold time would be between 3–5 years before changing direction.

In the words of Warren Buffett, “If you aren’t willing to own a stock for 10 years, don’t even think about owning it for 10 minutes.”
Risk
Positions are reviewed multiple times a week, and I exit when the long-term thesis no longer makes sense, whether the moat erodes, geopolitical shifts alter the landscape, or the underlying bottleneck is resolved. To prevent over-exposure, my largest single-firm position is capped at 5% of my portfolio.

Position size is weighted between conviction, risk, and the durability of the underlying trend, supported by analysis of the company's financial health using cash flow assessment, ratio analysis, and other KPIs.
What I look for
I am currently invested in AI compute/memory, biotech, precision medicine, energy transition, grid modernisation, and digital infrastructure.

Each firm I hold in those sectors solves a critical bottleneck, such as therapeutic innovation and drug discovery, clean hydrogen and energy storage, or memory bandwidth, because bottlenecks define where value must accrue. The firms are supported by durable moats built on IP, scientific capability, or manufacturing complexity, and must display healthy balance sheets and disciplined capital allocation that supports long-term compounding growth.

I also assess the leadership team, to see whether management is aligned with long-term value creation and understands the structural forces shaping their industry.
Discipline
I maintain discipline by writing an investment thesis before entering a position, based on the criteria above, and by setting out the bull and bear case to anchor decisions in logic rather than emotion. I set review dates to evaluate the thesis, rather than falling into the trap of market noise or sentiment swings. This keeps every decision grounded and deliberate.

Performance

Monthly returns are calculated from portfolio value at each month end, before fees, and are not adjusted for deposits or withdrawals made during the month.
